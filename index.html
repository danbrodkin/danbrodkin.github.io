Dan Brodkin

[dbrodkin@sas.upenn (dot) edu]

I'm a Postdoctoral Fellow in Syntax at the University of Pennsylvania. My research program is centered around the nature of extended projection and the broad architecture of PF. The core of my dissertation studies how heads are bundled and split in the TP and CP and proposes a derivational mechanism that unfolds the functional sequence; my earlier work studies the nature of ergativity and the A-A' divide. In my interface work, I have shown that prosodic phrasing can reveal functional projections in the VP and argued that prosodic constraints can trigger path containment effects and guide allomorph selection, linearization, chain reduction, and ellipsis.

An important part of my career involves supporting linguistics and training linguists in Indonesia. Much of my research is grounded in fieldwork on Mandar, a language of Sulawesi- and I believe that careful documentary work of this type can shed light on the most fundamental questions in generative theory.


I run the Penn Syntax Lab with Matt Hewett and Julie Legate. I completed my Ph.D at UCSC in 2025, working with Sandy Chung and Jim McCloskey.
